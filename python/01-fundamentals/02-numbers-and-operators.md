# Numbers and Operators

> **Status:** Syllabus outline. We will expand this lesson into teaching notes, a coding lab, exercises, and a small project when we study it.

## Learning objectives

- Use integer and floating-point values in arithmetic.
- Distinguish arithmetic, comparison, and logical operators.
- Predict expressions by applying operator precedence and parentheses.
- Explain floor division, modulo, and the difference between `/` and `//`.
- Recognize that floating-point arithmetic can have precision limits.

## Sections

1. **Numeric types** — `int` and `float`, and when each is useful.
2. **Arithmetic operators** — addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, floor division, modulo, and exponentiation.
3. **Precedence and grouping** — evaluation order and using parentheses for clarity.
4. **Comparison operators** — equal, not equal, greater/less than, and their inclusive forms.
5. **Augmented assignment** — forms such as `+=` and `*=`.
6. **Floating-point behavior** — rounding, representation limits, and why exact decimal equality can surprise beginners.
7. **Useful numeric tools** — a carefully scoped introduction to `abs()`, `round()`, and the `math` module.
8. **Engineering context** — units, meaningful names, and avoiding accidental unit mismatches.

## Coding lab

Calculate the average and difference of two measurements. Extend the script to calculate a percentage or convert between two compatible units, documenting the units in variable names or comments.

## Exercises

- Predict expression results before running them.
- Rewrite ambiguous expressions with parentheses.
- Choose between `/`, `//`, and `%` for a stated task.
- Explain a floating-point result that differs from an exact decimal expectation.

## Knowledge check

Explain operator precedence, floor division, modulo, and one limitation of floating-point values.
